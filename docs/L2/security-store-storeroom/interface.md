## 0. Scope, and what this document does not fix

Two modules' I/O surface and nothing beneath it. `src/main/operator-settings/` is the state machine — a separate `provident-storeroom` instance, its own lifetime and its own file, not the instance `provident.data.*` reads (D-16) — and `src/main/settings-surface/` sees it only through read, write and listeners (D-17). No MCP surface, resource, `RpcMethod` member or rendered element (D-18); the five `data.*` routes' own answer shapes are `central-data`'s (`docs/L2/central-data/interface.md` §5), and this unit settles only that the group does not gate them (§6).

## 1. The six members, the third address, and why the measurement decides

The record is **one value per tier, written whole**, so each transition is one store write, one op, one cursor and one receipt (L1 §3; `guide §4` arm `W-4`).

| Member | Tier | Address | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `token` · `enabled` · `maxJournalLength` · `entries` | `file` | `['operator-settings']` | the durable record, written whole: the bearer token or `null` (D-17); the granted group names, deduplicated through the **six**-member set (D-11, D-15); the supervisor's journal cap, or absent (D-17); the arbitrary-name map, **absent until first written** (D-16 behaviour carried) |
| `exclusion` · `tier4` | `mem` | `['operator-exclusion']` | `'mcp-enabled'` \| `'mcp-disabled'`, and its **derived** reading `exclusion === 'mcp-disabled'` — neither ever persisted (ASM-L2-2) |

- **Three addresses, and the measurement is why.** The record's four members stand at `['operator-settings']` on `file` and the pair at `['operator-exclusion']` on `mem` (L1 §3), and **the module mints a third**: the retired tier-1 carrier's app-state rows stand at `['operator-app-state']` on `file`, carried whole as `{rows:[{name,value},…]}` — an ordered array, never a name→value map — written by `writeAppState` and re-projected by `readAppState` with **no listener**, because `subscribe` binds the two addresses in the table above (the `APP_STATE_ADDRESS` constant and both verbs in `src/main/operator-settings/index.ts`; `docs/L1/security-store-storeroom-exception.md` §6; `docs/L2/settings-carrier-retirement/interface.md` §1; `persistence.md` §7). **A `file`-tier write purges the versions it outranks at its own key** (`file` > `mem` > `temp`), so no durable commit can reset another address: that is why the pair cannot share a durable key and why the app-state rows are a key of their own rather than a member of the record's value (`guide §5.1`; `persistence.md` §9 P-3). `ASM-L2-1` records the re-grain and what it rejected (D-17, D-22, L1 §3).
- **A patch names one tier, and it names at least one member of it**: the four `file` members, **or** `exclusion`. A patch naming **both** is outside this domain, because it is two store writes and therefore two receipts or a landed-then-refused tear (`ASM-L2-5`); a patch naming **neither** — `write({})`, the empty patch the all-optional `OperatorSettingsPatch` admits by its type — is refused the same way. **All three shape arms refuse as values, before any store verb**: a patch that is not a plain object, one naming both tiers, and one naming neither each answer `{status:'refused', reason:'ValueRefusedAtBoundary', message:<the module's own sentence>, address:null}` — the third with *the patch names no member of either tier* — so an empty patch is a **refusal and not a no-op**, and the type's optional members do not make it legal (the `write` body's three pre-tier refusals in `src/main/operator-settings/index.ts`; `behaviour.md` §4; D-16). **Each of its five member-value arms refuses the same way, with its own sentence** (§3's row carries all eight and their nine sites): `exclusion` must be one of the two literals; a `token` that is neither `null` nor a `string` refuses — and **`''` is the landed CLEAR, not a refusal**, which is what makes that sentence say *non-empty*; `enabled` must be an array, its non-group strings being **dropped** rather than refused (§2); `entries` must be a plain object, not an array and not `null`; and `maxJournalLength` follows §2's order exactly.

It answers the **first-run default** over no file (`AddressNotFound`) and the **safe pair** over no `mem` version (`TierNotFound`) — both read arms, neither a refusal (§3; `behaviour.md` §1, §7); **and since D-84 the `mem` half is no longer a boot's**: the declaration at `['operator-exclusion']` stands at the store's creation and repairs absence to `{exclusion:'mcp-enabled'}`, so the boot reads a pair the constraint minted and `TierNotFound` is reached only by a NON-TOKEN member or a refused read — the two arms `SAFE_PAIR` still answers. **Since D-85 the `file` half is no boot's either**: the record address and the row-list address are declared too, and their repairs PERFORM the imports that addresses' absence used to key, so `read()`'s first-run arm is reached only by a refused construction — no instance, hence no batch and no repair. A **cleared member is written as its absence** — `token:null` and a `null` cap are the patch's clear markers and never stored values — because the package's walk refuses `null` and `undefined` anywhere in a value (`guide §10.1`, `§10.2`); `read()` maps absence back to `null`/`undefined` for the carrier's byte-identical `SecuritySettings` (L1 §4). **The cap's landed clear/refuse split is carried verbatim, and its check ORDER is the rule**: a `null` cap clears; a non-number refuses; then **`NaN` or any value `<= 0` clears — and `-Infinity` is `<= 0`, so the minus arm CLEARS and commits with the cap absent**; only a value surviving all three fails `Number.isFinite` and refuses, so **`+Infinity` alone** answers `ValueRefusedAtBoundary` with **this module's own sentence, `'maxJournalLength is a finite number or null'`**, and no store verb runs; a positive finite value commits **floored** (`Math.floor`) — the order is why the sign decides (the `write` body's cap arm in `src/main/operator-settings/index.ts`; `behaviour.md` §2, §4).
- **`write()` answers a receipt and never throws**, for any patch and any state (L1 §3) — and **its domain is §1's**: a plain object naming one tier's members, at least one, so an empty patch refuses (`ValueRefusedAtBoundary`, `address:null`) rather than committing a no-op. **`cursor` is the committed op's own `seq` and `opId` its own string, taken verbatim** (the declaration's `JournalOp`; `guide §6.4`): the module adds no offset and renumbers nothing, **a fresh instance's first commit answers the FOURTH ordinal, because the boot's three creation repairs are the journal's first three ops (`D-85`)** — and the rows assert the ordinal as an integer and by difference, with the one absolute figure they pin being that creation instant's (`ASM-L2-7`; `D-84` measured `2`, the head before it `1`).
- **`enabled` is deduplicated first-occurrence-first, in the order the caller or the imported file supplied it**, a non-group string dropped: the landed rule carried as behaviour and never re-sorted into `VALID_GROUPS` order, so a caller writing `['data','read','dispatch']` is served exactly that (the deleted `src/main/security-store.ts`'s dedup site; D-11, D-15; A-5).

## 2.1 The construction act, the file, and the import-once path

`createOperatorSettings` is a **declared act with a declared disk effect**, not an invisible one, because the battery that drives it reads the disk (L1 §5; `persistence.md` §2).

- **The file is `<userDataDir>/provident-operator-settings.json`**, `userDataDir` being the module's only construction input besides the optional journal cap (§2). **The store's construction creates it exclusively, zero-length and empty** where it is absent, and **truncates nothing** where it stands (`guide §9.1`; D-13, ASM-L2-6). **Nothing else is created beside it** — no `.tmp`, no lock, no sidecar and no second written name, at construction or at any later write (`persistence.md` §5; D-16) — and **the boot writes NO BYTE of the file**: construction, the three creation repairs and `attach` land journal ops and materialise nothing — the package saves at a `file`-tier write's commit and at a restore that moved (`guide §9.3`), so the file stands at the zero length the exclusive create gave it until the first caller write (§5; `persistence.md` §2; measured 2026-11-03, D-85).
- **The import reads `<userDataDir>/provident-security.json` INSIDE the repair at `['operator-settings']`, at the store's creation, and again at every boot whose record address carries nothing** — it runs **iff** the instance's own file carries no record at that address, so the once-per-profile bound belongs to the **write** a committed record stands on and never to the read — and it **never writes, renames, repairs or deletes** the retired file: its bytes stand as seeded and the retired name is retired (D-19; L1 §5; D-85; `behaviour.md` §3; `persistence.md` §3). Its six arms are the file absent, an object, unparsable, a non-object, a directory/unreadable, and `entries` not a plain object; the object arm is written into the record as `toStored(imported)`, the `entries` arm that record with `entries` treated as absent, and every other arm writes `toStored(FIRST_RUN)` — the constant the read used to substitute — so a repair never mints a placeholder over an import, none throwing (the `mintRecord` repair in `src/main/operator-settings/index.ts`; `behaviour.md` §3; D-85).
- **A directory or name the store cannot occupy refuses the construction without throwing**: no instance stands, no file and no byte is created, `read()` answers the first-run default, and **every later `write` answers `FilePathUnreachable`** (`guide §8.2`; `behaviour.md` §4).

## 3. The receipt vocabulary, and the reason set that carries an escape arm

A refusal is a **value on this surface**: the store's own status verbatim as `reason`, the package's own generated sentence as `message`, and the package's fourth member as `address` (`guide §8`, `§6.5a`; the declaration's `StoreRefusal`). **The set is OPEN, and it must be**: the store's own `StoreStatus` ends in an **open `(string & {})` escape arm** whose declaration calls that arm *"load-bearing rather than loosness"* — the set has moved five times, and an immutably-enumerated union *"would mis-type a LAWFUL refusal the moment the set moves again"* (`index.d.ts` `StoreStatus`; `guide §8.1`). So this surface **carries any status the store crosses verbatim** under the escape arm and **narrows on three named spellings only**, with the battery's relation to each stated rather than implied: **two are spelled by a row** — `ValueRefusedAtBoundary` and `FilePathUnreachable` — and the third, `FileCommitFailed`, is driven as an **arm** (`SC-E5-08`) and spelled by **no** row, which is a known gap and not a covered claim (`docs/L1/security-store-storeroom-scenarios.md` §6). The three:

| `reason` | Rank | The arms that produce it |
|---|---|---|
| `ValueRefusedAtBoundary` | 17 | the write carries a value the crossing refuses **before any store verb** — a function, a symbol, `undefined`, a cycle, a non-finite number, or a node past the two ceilings (`guide §10.1-§10.3`) — **and eight sentences this module mints itself, at nine sites**, each answering the same word with no store verb at all: §1's three shape arms (*the patch is not a plain object*; *the patch names both tiers, and a patch names one*; *the patch names no member of either tier*), and its five member-value arms — `'exclusion is mcp-enabled or mcp-disabled'`, `'token is a non-empty string or null'`, `'enabled is an array of group names'`, `'entries is a plain object of names to values'`, and `'maxJournalLength is a finite number or null'` **at two sites**, the non-number arm and the non-finite one (§1, §2; the `write` body in `src/main/operator-settings/index.ts`) |
| `FileCommitFailed` | 15 | a `file`-tier save cannot land; the store's journal rollback has already run, so the previous bytes stand (`guide §9.4`). **The arm is driven and the word is not**: `SC-E5-08` asserts a returned `refused` value and byte-identical bytes without pinning the status (`docs/L1/security-store-storeroom-scenarios.md` §6) |
| `FilePathUnreachable` | 13 | the store's construction refused, so no instance stands and each later write answers it (`guide §8.2`) |

- **`ContainerKindConflict` is NOT excluded — it is unnamed, and the earlier ground for excluding it was wrong.** It is reachable in principle whenever the boot leaves a container at a key whose kind a later whole-value write contradicts (`guide §4` arm `F-4`), and that boot arm is undeclared (`persistence.md` §9 P-1). It therefore rides the escape arm verbatim like every other unnamed status; what separates it from the three above is that **no row drives it**, which is a statement about this battery and not about the store (D-5, `ASM-L2-3`). **The other unnamed status is not a store status at all**: a caught value carrying no `status` (`guide §8.3`) is contained and answered `reason:'UnattributedFailure'` with its own message — a literal under the escape arm — and **no row drives it**, because the one declared unattributed throw site is `write`'s `commit` position, which this module never supplies (`guide §3.3`).
- **`'write-failed'` and `'tier4-closed'` do not survive as write-outcome tokens** (D-16); `'tier4-closed'` survives only as the **carrier's read token** (§5). **A refusal leaves the record and the bytes exactly as they stood and mints no cursor** (L1 §3; `guide §9.4`).

## 5.1 The transition answer carries no receipt, and the refusal rides the `set` channel

**Resolved one way, and said out loud: `SettingsTransitionAnswer` gains no `write` member.** The bridge's own declaration is the ground — `setExclusion` is declared `Promise<{applied: boolean; state: EXCLUSION_STATE; reason?: 'malformed-state'}>` and the receipt member `write` is declared **only** on `set` (the `set` and `setExclusion` members of the bridge's `SecuritySettings` block in `src/main/preload.ts`) — so declaring a receipt on the transition would reshape a bridge answer that **E-2 does not licence** and would need `secure-panels.ts`'s `declare global` widened in lockstep (exception §2 E-2, §6; D-18).

- **The receipt of a transition is observed on the `set` channel and on the record**, never on the transition's own answer: `set(...).write` carries it, and `read().exclusion` carries the state the transition left (`behaviour.md` §10; §5).
