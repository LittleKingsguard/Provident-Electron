## 3. The drag composition's contract (landed, and unmoved by this pass)

- **Two phases, two tiers, one datum's owner** (D-132, D-138): the move writes `['drag']` at `temp` once per placement change and commits nothing; the release runs the sink, then `view.commitPane(DEFAULT_PANE_ID, { height: final })` — the ADJUSTABLE member, `pane-a` being stage-resident and therefore sidebar-equivalent, `C-12`/`D-189` — at `['panes']` · `file`, then `view.clearDrag()`; the erase turn sends `clearDrag` alone. **One ending per gesture**, kept by ONE bookkeeping flag cleared by `move` and set by whichever ending turn ends the reading first (`['drag']` is keyed by pane id and is gesture-blind, `F-6`).
- **Both drag writes seat at the affordance's ONE preview seam**, and its own `PreviewState.valid` flag is the discriminator — `valid: true` is the placement change, `valid: false` (the revert `revertStateFor` builds) is the ending; **total: no counter arithmetic and no second listener** (`D-126`'s class).
- **The gesture's member IS the member the release commits** (rev 13, `D-189`, `D-190`): the move writes `['drag']` at `temp` as `{ [DEFAULT_PANE_ID]: { height } }` and the release commits `{ height: final }`, so the ghost's reading, the read-out's figure and the stored record carry **one member name** — the pane's adjustable one — and nothing converts between them (`D-136`'s seat, extended from formatting to naming).
- **The release hands `release` NO sink** (`D-129`, `D-126`'s `F3`): the read-out is the DELIVERY's, painted by `renderPaneSize` from `paneSizeOf(DEFAULT_PANE_ID)` — **which answers that pane's ADJUSTABLE member, its `height`** (`D-189`), the record authoritative and the gesture's clamped figure never its author. `R3` keeps its position and the seam its signature, because the demo realm still supplies one (D-118).
- **`pane-a`'s SUBJECT IS UNCHANGED** (D-183, `D-189`): the family widened for a SECOND id and `D-189` re-spells this pane's stored members rather than replacing its subject, so this composition's route, its two phases and its rows keep their subject and need no re-grain — which is the stated reason that arm was chosen; **what moves is the member NAME the release commits and the gesture records**, and that is §3's bullet above rather than a change of subject.

## 4. The ghost's contract — the delivery's reading, never a member of its own

ONE `data.read({path: ['drag']})` through the existing `dataRoute` pass-through, inside the turn the view record's push caused; the answer's node and that node's own `_tier` decide — the declaration is painted iff the node is a branch whose `_tier` is `temp`, naming the pane the release commits with **a finite non-negative member whose NAME is the axis the gesture moves** — `{ height }` for `pane-a`, `{ width }` for a `top`/`bottom` pane, one member and no second (`D-189`, `D-190`; the read's own contract is `D-133`/`D-144`). It paints ONE declared transient attribute on the pane element the wiring already holds: no graph write, no element created, no re-render mid-gesture. **EVERY ARM IS CLOSED BUT ONE, AND THE EXCEPTION IS STATED**: no `data` member, a thrown route, `absent`, a ledger arm, a foreign `_tier`, another pane's member, a member naming an axis this pane does not adjust — **a `size` member is one of these, since it names no axis at all** — and a non-finite figure each REMOVE the declaration and throw nothing — while **a target that will not take an attribute RETURNS ahead of the paint and removes nothing**, so such a target is one this turn can never clear (ASM-RW-3). A delivery that never arrives paints nothing; a delivery lost after one was painted leaves a STALE ghost, never a fabricated one (`D-117`), and a superseded turn (each carries an epoch) paints nothing.

## 5. The record's act, and the ONE body that now decides both pages

- **The act's write is ONE ROW and the record's repairs own every other byte** (D-158, D-159): `{path: ['tabs', <the row's own id>], tier: 'file', value: <that row>}`, the row WHOLE with `active: true` and `lastActiveAt` stamped — **no `order` is written**, because `order-names-its-tabs` seats it and `exactly-one-active` deactivates the row that stood active. **THE TWO ARMS DIFFER IN THE ROW'S MEMBERS, AND THAT IS THE CONTRACT**: an act over a STANDING row writes that row as the reading gave it; an act that OPENS one writes `{id, target, active: true, lastActiveAt}` and **neither `label` nor `error`** — the first because the record's own field pairs fill it, the second because `error` is optional and no repair supplies it, so a tab that has not failed carries none. The after-read stays owed to the ANSWER (D-159).
- **ONE subscription body takes the push, and the push carries an ADDRESS and never a value** (D-137, D-142): the client has already RE-READ when the body runs, so the re-read is the only correct answer to it and the body composes nothing. **From this rev that body also MINTS** (§2, turn 1) — in that order, the reading first and the mint on it, never the reverse.
- **THE WITNESS DRIVE IS RE-GRAINED ONTO THE PAINT'S ONE BODY** (`C-4`): `driveTabsLandingPage` gives each authored terminal its `css.style` `display` through `drivePageDisplay`, and after this rev the STAGE's paint is that same body — resolved `target`, one `state-slice`, one author for both pages (`D-92`, `D-76`, `D-129`). **`DC-1`…`DC-5` re-grain onto it in the same edit** and report on the new middle rather than beside it.
- **What is driven is the REPAIR's answer**: a reading that is not `committed` drives NOTHING — the envelope's authored `display` (or the last record's word) stands, and a realm that has heard nothing renders nothing it was not authored with (`D-127`).
